# Career Onboarding

## Purpose

Build a comprehensive career profile — history, skills and career capital, values, market position, goals, and risk posture — enabling AI to act as a strategic career advisor: weighing decisions, managing optionality and downside risk, and developing positioning and public presence.

Informed by a synthesis of evidence-based career frameworks: Reid Hoffman's *The Start-up of You* (competitive advantage, ABZ planning, permanent beta, network intelligence), the [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/career-guide/) career framework (career capital, personal fit, decision-making under uncertainty), Cal Newport's career-capital theory (*So Good They Can't Ignore You*), [Designing Your Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designing_Your_Life) (Burnett & Evans, Stanford — Workview/Lifeview, Odyssey Plans, energy auditing), and Naval Ravikant's model of specific knowledge and leverage. Grounded academically in Krumboltz's planned-happenstance theory (turning chance into opportunity).

## Prerequisites

Root profile must be complete. Reference it for identity, location, basic work situation, finances, and stated goals — do not re-ask what is already recorded. This onboarding goes deeper on professional specifics and strategy.

## Instructions

This is a strategic intake, not a form. Conduct it as a natural conversation — listen more than you talk, and probe where it matters most: the user's genuine strengths (past modesty or inflation), their real risk exposure and runway, and the gap between their current trajectory and where they want to be.

Career advice is only as good as the honesty behind it. Make room for uncertainty — many people have never articulated their aspirations or fallback plans, and helping them think it through is part of the value. Where the user has already given relevant context elsewhere in the system, fold it in rather than re-asking.

Three framework lenses run through the questions; keep them in mind:
- **Competitive advantage** (Hoffman) = assets × aspirations/values × market realities. All three legs matter; strength in one without the others is fragile.
- **Career capital** (Newport, 80,000 Hours) — rare, valuable, transferable skills, plus connections, reputation, and financial runway — is the currency spent to buy better options later.
- **Optionality** (Hoffman's ABZ, planned happenstance) — the aim is not a single fixed plan but a strong Plan A, live pivots (Plan B), a safe fallback (Plan Z), and a wide surface area for luck.

## Questions

### Career history and track record
- Walk me through your career so far — roles, companies, durations, and what you actually did or built in each.
- What have you shipped, built, or achieved that you're most proud of? (Look for concrete artifacts and outcomes, not job titles.)
- What's your education, and any credentials or training that matter?
- How did you get into your field, and what shaped the path?

### Skills and career capital
- What are you genuinely, rarely good at? (Probe for "specific knowledge" — skills that can't be easily trained or outsourced.)
- What's your edge — what can you do that most of your peers can't?
- Where are you weak, or dependent on others?
- What's publicly visible as proof of your ability, and what's hidden (private repos, NDA'd work, unattributed output)?
- How strong is your professional network, and which communities or circles are you part of?
- What's your financial runway — how many months could you sustain yourself with no income? (This is career capital: it sets how much risk you can take.)

### Assets, aspirations, and values
- Soft assets: skills, knowledge, connections, reputation. Hard assets: savings, equity, equipment.
- What do you want your work to mean? What makes work "good" for you beyond pay? (Workview)
- What are you optimising for right now — money, freedom, mastery, impact, status, security, or optionality? Rank them honestly.
- What does a well-lived life look like to you, and how does work serve it? (Lifeview)

### Market realities
- Who pays for what you do, and how much? Where is demand heading?
- How is your field changing — particularly the effect of AI on it?
- What makes you differentiated in that market, versus interchangeable?

### Current situation and satisfaction
- How satisfied are you with your current role and trajectory? What's working, and what isn't?
- Which parts of your work energise you, and which drain you? (Energy/engagement audit.)
- Are you spending energy fighting any "gravity problems" — real constraints you can't change and should design around rather than resist?

### Direction and odyssey plans
- Where would you like to be in five years? Sketch a few genuinely different versions: your current path extended, a deliberate pivot, and a wildcard with no constraints.
- Is there a longer-term direction or mission pulling you, or are you still exploring?
- What would make the next few years a wild success?

### Risk, optionality, and ABZ planning
- How much risk are you currently carrying? (e.g., single income source, concentrated equity, dependence on one employer or client.)
- **Plan A** — what you're doing now. **Plan B** — what you'd pivot to if A fails or a better opportunity appears, and what would trigger the switch. **Plan Z** — your fallback if everything fails, the floor you can always retreat to.
- What's the realistic downside scenario, and over what timeline could it unfold?

### Leverage and public presence
- What forms of leverage do you have or want — labour (people), capital, code/products, or media/audience? (The last two scale without permission.)
- What's your current public footprint and reputation — online presence, audience, portfolio, body of public work?
- How do opportunities currently reach you — do you chase them, or do they find you? How could you widen that surface area for luck?
- Are you building anything that compounds over time (an audience, products, equity, a reputation in a niche)?

### Constraints and preferences
- What constrains your choices — location, remote/in-person, lifestyle, partner, family, visa, relocation plans? (Reference the root profile.)
- How do you prefer to work, and what kind of environment brings out your best?
- What do you want from AI career support — a strategic sounding board, accountability, opportunity scanning, positioning help, decision analysis, or all of these?

## After onboarding

Save stable data to **profile.md** under these headings:
- **History** — roles, companies, durations, what was built; education and credentials
- **Skills and career capital** — rare/valuable skills, specific knowledge, edge, weaknesses; proof points (visible and hidden); network and communities
- **Competitive advantage** — assets, aspirations/values, and market realities (Hoffman's triad), and the differentiation that falls out of their intersection
- **Direction** — five-year odyssey sketches, longer-term mission, definition of success
- **Leverage and public presence** — leverage held and sought, current footprint, what's being built to compound
- **Preferences and constraints** — work style, environment, hard constraints, and what they want from AI career support

Record volatile state in **status.md** (the single source of truth for what changes):
- **Current role and terms** — title, equity/comp, stipend, status of the venture or employer
- **Runway** — months of financial cushion (update as it changes)
- **Active plans (ABZ)** — current Plan A/B/Z and the triggers that would move between them, including any known time horizon for a forced change
- **Opportunity pipeline** — live conversations, leads, applications, or inbound interest
- **Public-presence experiments** — current channel, cadence, lane, and what's working
- **Decisions in flight** — choices currently being weighed

Keep stable history, skills, values, and preferences in profile.md; anything that shifts month to month — runway, current terms, active plans, pipeline — belongs in status.md. For significant career events (a job change, a major decision, a venture outcome), consider an `events/` entry dated `YYYY-MM-DD`.
