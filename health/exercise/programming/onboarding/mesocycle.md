# Mesocycle Onboarding

Playbook for planning a mesocycle (3-6 weeks). Run this as an AI-mediated conversation to produce a mesocycle README.

## Theoretical basis

- **Volume Landmarks** (Israetel, Renaissance Periodization) — each muscle group has a Minimum Effective Volume (MEV), Maximum Adaptive Volume (MAV), and Maximum Recoverable Volume (MRV). Programming should start near MEV and progress toward MAV across the block, never exceeding MRV.
- **Progressive Overload** — the fundamental driver of adaptation. Can be achieved via load, volume, density (less rest), or range of motion. At least one variable must progress week-to-week.
- **Specificity (SAID Principle)** — adaptations are specific to the demands imposed. Exercise selection must match the training goal (strength requires heavy compounds; hypertrophy requires mechanical tension near failure).
- **Strategic Variation** (Zatsiorsky & Kraemer, 2006) — sufficient variation to prevent accommodation, but not so much that adaptation is scattered. Vary accessories across blocks; keep primary lifts consistent for tracking.
- **Autoregulation** (Helms et al., 2016; Zourdos et al., 2021) — RPE/RIR-based load prescription accommodates daily readiness and avoids rigid percentage-based programming that ignores fatigue state.
- **Minimum Effective Dose for Maintenance** (Bickel et al., 2011) — qualities not being actively developed can be maintained with ~1/3 of the volume that built them. This frees recovery budget for the block's primary focus.

## Prerequisites

Before running this onboarding:

- The parent macrocycle README exists (goals, phase plan, timeline)
- The athlete's current state is known (from profiles and recent feedback)
- If a previous mesocycle exists in this macrocycle, its review section is populated

## Onboarding steps

### 1. Context review

Gather:

- What phase is this in the macrocycle? (e.g., "Mesocycle 2 of 4: Strength I")
- What is this block's role? (from macrocycle phase plan)
- What did the previous mesocycle achieve? (read review if available)
- What feedback patterns emerged? (scan workout feedback from previous block)
- Are there new constraints since the macrocycle was planned? (injury changes, schedule changes, motivation state)

### 2. Block focus definition

Define clearly:

**Primary training quality** (1-2 max):

- Hypertrophy (muscle growth)
- Maximal strength (1-5RM capacity)
- Power (force × velocity)
- Work capacity / aerobic base
- Skill acquisition (gymnastics, Olympic lifting technique)

**Secondary quality** (maintenance dose):

- What needs to be preserved from previous blocks?
- What conditioning dose maintains the aerobic base?
- What gymnastics skill practice continues?

**Key movements for this block:**

- 3-5 primary lifts that will be tracked for progression (e.g., back squat, bench press, strict press)
- These should remain consistent across the block for measurable overload
- Selection should serve the block's primary quality and the macrocycle's goals

### 3. Training split design

Distribute work across the athlete's available training days.

**Common split options (5-day example, adapt for other frequencies):**

| Split                            | Structure                        | Best for                      |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Upper/Lower/Push/Pull/Full       | Varied daily stimulus            | General strength with variety |
| Push/Pull/Legs/Upper/Lower       | High frequency per pattern       | Hypertrophy and strength      |
| Strength/Hypertrophy alternating | Heavy/light days                 | Undulating within block       |
| Primary lift focus               | Each day built around 1 big lift | Strength intensification      |
| Movement pattern                 | Squat/Hinge/Push/Pull/Mixed      | Balanced development          |

**Considerations for split selection:**

- Frequency: how many times per week each muscle group is trained (2-3x optimal for hypertrophy; 2-4x for strength depending on intensity)
- Recovery: 48-72h between training the same muscle group heavy
- Interference: don't place heavy conditioning before a strength day for the same patterns
- Enjoyment: vary the daily experience enough to prevent monotony
- Injury management: distribute stress on vulnerable joints across the week
- Gymnastics/skill: place early in sessions when fresh, or on lighter days

### 4. Volume and intensity planning

**Volume (sets per muscle group per week):**

- Start at or slightly above MEV in week 1
- Progress by 1-2 sets per muscle group per week
- Reach near MAV by the final training week
- Deload at ~50-60% of peak volume

General volume guidelines for trained individuals (sets/week):

- Large muscle groups (quads, back, chest): 10-20 sets/week at maintenance, up to 20-25 when prioritised
- Small muscle groups (biceps, triceps, delts): 8-16 sets/week
- Prioritised weakness: allocate toward upper end of range
- Maintenance qualities: reduce to ~6-10 sets/week (1/3 of development dose)

**Intensity (load relative to capacity):**

- Define RPE/RIR targets per exercise category:
  - Primary lifts: RPE targets by week (e.g., W1: RPE 7, W2: RPE 7.5, W3: RPE 8, W4: RPE 8.5)
  - Accessories: generally RPE 7-9 (1-3 RIR), progressing across the block
  - Conditioning: by effort level (easy/moderate/hard) rather than RPE

**Rep ranges by block type:**

- Hypertrophy: 6-12 reps primary, 8-15 accessories
- Strength: 3-5 reps primary, 6-8 accessories (maintain muscle)
- Power: 1-3 reps primary (explosive), 6-10 accessories
- Work capacity: 8-15+ reps, shorter rest, higher density

**Weekly progression model:**
Define how load/volume increases week-to-week. Common approaches:

- Linear: add fixed weight each week (e.g., +2.5 kg upper, +5 kg lower)
- RPE-matched: keep RPE target constant, add weight when previous week felt easier
- Volume wave: increase sets week-to-week, reset volume on deload
- Double progression: hit top of rep range → increase weight, drop to bottom of range

### 5. Exercise selection

For each training day, select:

**Primary lift (1-2):** Compound, trackable, serves the block's focus. These stay consistent across the block. Examples: back squat, bench press, deadlift, strict press, front squat.

**Accessories (2-4):** Support the primary lift or address weaknesses. Can rotate across the block for variety. Select based on:

- What muscle groups need more volume?
- What weaknesses limit the primary lift?
- What movement patterns are underrepresented?
- What does the athlete enjoy? (adherence matters)

**Conditioning (where applicable):** Select modality and format based on the block's conditioning goal:

- Maintenance: 1-2x/week, moderate effort, 15-25 min
- Development: 3-4x/week, varied energy systems, structured intervals
- Sport-specific: CrossFit-style mixed modal, heavy WODs

**Gymnastics/skill (where applicable):**

- Placed when fresh (start of session or dedicated lighter day)
- Focus on 1-2 skills per block
- Progressive: build from easier to harder variations

**Warm-up/rehab:**

- Consistent warm-up protocol (see microcycle playbook for RAMP details)
- Rehab/prehab exercises for managed injuries (band work, core, mobility)

### 6. Deload week design

The final week of each mesocycle is a deload. Purpose: dissipate accumulated fatigue while maintaining fitness.

**Deload parameters:**

- Volume: 50-60% of peak week (halve the sets, keep exercises)
- Intensity: maintain or slightly reduce (same weight, fewer sets — OR — reduce weight 10%, same sets)
- No conditioning tests or max efforts
- Can include extra mobility, recovery work, light skill practice
- Psychologically: a break from pushing hard — not "easy" but "not draining"

### 7. Injury accommodations

For each active injury/restriction:

- What movements are affected?
- What modifications are needed? (substitute, reduce load, reduce ROM)
- What rehab work should be included?
- Are there progression criteria? (e.g., "if pain-free for 2 weeks, attempt X")

Cross-reference: health/exercise/status.md (current restrictions and rehab stages) and relevant issues/ files.

### 8. Success criteria

Define what a successful block looks like:

- Load progression targets (e.g., "add 5-10 kg to primary lifts across the block")
- Volume completion (e.g., "complete prescribed volume without excessive fatigue")
- Subjective markers (e.g., "consistently rate sessions RPE 7-8, not 9-10")
- Skill milestones (e.g., "achieve 3 unbroken strict HSPU by end of block")
- No injury flare-ups
